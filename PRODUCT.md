# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

- Product, design, and engineering leadership evaluating judgment, systems thinking, and proof of work.
- Recruiters and hiring teams who need a fast read on role fit, seniority, and credibility.
- Collaborators who want to understand Fadly's operating model before starting a project.
- People evaluating whether the portfolio demonstrates real B2B complexity, not just polished screens.

## Purpose

The Human Algorithm Portfolio is an interactive architecture portfolio where the impression is the product. It should prove that Fadly can design resilient product systems, handle trust-sensitive B2B workflows, communicate tradeoffs, protect accessibility and performance, and turn complex product work into understandable evidence.

The site should feel like an operating system for proof of work: fast, intentional, inspectable, and human enough that the craft does not disappear behind the system.

## Brand Personality

- System-driven: every page should feel backed by a clear model, not decorative improvisation.
- Human: the work should carry judgment, care, and lived product context.
- Resilient: interactions, layout, and copy should keep working under real-world constraints.
- Precise: details should be specific, measurable where useful, and free of vague positioning.

## What The Portfolio Must Prove

- Product design judgment across discovery, IA, flows, prototypes, and execution details.
- Trust engineering: clear handling of risk, constraints, operational ambiguity, and user confidence.
- B2B complexity handling: workflows, data density, edge cases, stakeholder needs, and long-lived systems.
- Accessibility craft: readable type, keyboard support, focus states, contrast, and reduced-motion care.
- Performance craft: responsive pages, bounded effects, lazy loading, and spectacle that earns its cost.

## Anti-References

- Generic portfolio templates that reduce the work to cards, timelines, and vague "selected work" framing.
- Decorative AI glow, bokeh, or gradient spectacle that does not explain the work.
- Unreadable motion-heavy art sites where aesthetics compete with comprehension.
- Vague "creative technologist" positioning without concrete product evidence.
- Performance-costly spectacle that makes recruiters wait, janks mobile devices, or hides content.

## Success Criteria

- A first-time visitor can identify Fadly's positioning, strongest evidence, and next step within one minute.
- Leadership can trace work from product context to decision, system, and outcome.
- Recruiter mode and CV paths feel like first-class routes, not secondary downloads.
- Every core route preserves the same strategic identity while allowing page-specific rhythm.
- The site remains responsive, accessible, and readable across mobile, tablet, and desktop.
